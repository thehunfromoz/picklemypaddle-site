Every pickleball paddle starts life with a textured face. That texture is what lets the paddle "bite" the ball, so you can put spin on serves, dinks and drives.

With every rally, the ball rubs against that surface. Over months of regular play the texture wears down, usually first in the sweet spot where you hit most often.

## Signs your paddle face is worn

- **It looks shiny** in the middle of the face, compared with the edges.
- **It feels smooth** when you run your fingers across the sweet spot.
- **Your spin has dropped off.** Shots that used to dip or kick now float.

## Your options

You can live with it, buy a new paddle, or have the face re-gritted. If you like how your paddle plays (its weight, balance and feel), re-gritting keeps all of that and restores the texture.

That's the idea behind Pickle My Paddle: preserve a good paddle rather than retire it.
